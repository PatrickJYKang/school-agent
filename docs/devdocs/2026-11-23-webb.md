# Webb context and student reflections

November 23, 2026. The project will continue as a restrained LibreChat fork and a
candidate for WebbGPT's next version. The next work centers on academic activities,
visible assistance boundaries, student control and inspectable evidence. The
[scope and priorities](../scope.md) translate this decision into implementation
and study work.

## Evidence and interpretation

Dr. Mark Dzula's _AI Self-Portraits: Themes from 93 Freshman Reflections_, dated
November 19, 2026, summarizes handwritten responses using manual transcription
and a keyword pass. It identifies studying, writing, brainstorming and research
as useful applications, alongside questions about permitted help, teachers' own
AI use, privacy, creative ownership and environmental costs. Several students
ask specifically about guidance that helps them reach an answer.

This is directional input from freshmen, not a representative survey of every
Webb student or a validated product-demand study. Themes overlap. A preference
count for WebbGPT cannot establish its total use. Different boundaries around
writing, creativity and personal information should inform choices rather than
be converted into a claim of unanimous preferences. The supplied PDF is not
copied into the repository; original worksheets and transcripts were not part of
this review.

Webb's [mission](https://www.webb.org/about/mission) emphasizes independent
thinking, honor, relationships and a boarding experience grounded in independence
and trust. Its [Dynamic Education framework](https://www.webb.org/the-future-of-learning/dynamic-education)
explicitly includes student involvement in designing education, emerging
technologies and ethical responsibility. The product implication is to involve
students in setting and evaluating its boundaries alongside educators, while
preserving their control over assistance and connected sources.

Webb's [humanities program](https://www.webb.org/academics/humanities-at-webb)
combines foundational reading and writing with specialized study, including a
_Literature & the Machine_ course that experiments with AI. Its
[science program](https://www.webb.org/academics/science-at-webb) emphasizes
inquiry, experiments and field research. These support different kinds of
delegation: feedback on a student's argument, help locating evidence, and
inspectable calculations for a research question. The learning objective should
determine which work a student retains.

Webb already [describes WebbGPT](https://www.webb.org/news-detail?fromId=295784&nc=25090&pk=1645389)
as supporting equitable AI access within faculty and student experimentation.
The project should demonstrate what a successor improves through comparisons
with that service and participants' usual tools. These public descriptions
express institutional direction; actual assistance rules for a course or
assignment must come from the school and responsible teacher.

## Scope decisions

1. **Organize around academic work.** Start with assignment/study help,
   reading/revision and a bounded data/research activity. Browser and Python
   tools serve these activities. Faculty preparation remains useful within the
   same source and disclosure principles.
2. **Show the teacher's guidance.** Keep existing written assistance guidance
   with the assignment and rubric. Say when it is missing. Help choices should
   include hints, practice and critique alongside capable ordinary chat. Avoid a
   universal tutoring restriction or a new course-policy management system.
3. **Make delegation visible.** Show actual sources, retrieval limits, code,
   results and relevant tool activity. Provide reliable Stop controls and a
   short, reviewed AI-use summary. A disclosure record must not certify
   authorship or reveal hidden model reasoning. Support equivalent transparency
   in teacher preparation.
4. **Preserve agency and trust.** Connections are explicit, personal memory is
   off by default in the intended study profile, and conversation access and
   retention are disclosed. Creative and personal work remain matters of user
   choice within activity rules. Companion positioning, surveillance, AI
   detection and automated grading are outside the scope.
5. **Earn use through quality.** Keep normal LibreChat chat and evaluate a small
   approved model choice. Explain selection and budget decisions. Do not assume
   school branding or helper templates alone will attract students already using
   commercial assistants.
6. **Sequence integrations by demonstrated need.** Use existing Canvas,
   document and public research capabilities first. Advance isolated Python
   through a selected task. Selected OneDrive/SharePoint files may follow;
   mailbox access and broader device/agent orchestration are deferred.

Environmental questions belong in the explanation of institutional choices and
the study discussion. Prefer bounded, purposeful work and clear usage information;
do not invent precise water or carbon estimates for individual interactions.

## Evaluation and follow through

The proposed initial study involves two or three faculty members and about
12-20 students, including skeptics and people who prefer other tools. Agree
activities, assistance boundaries, participation and data handling before study
use. Evaluate understanding, source and calculation checks, clarity of
delegation, usefulness, repeat-use interest, teacher workload and operating cost.
The study is exploratory; these inputs do not prove that the application improves
learning.

The existing technical boundaries remain: per-user read-only sources, institution
authorization enforced outside the model, portable Markdown working material,
isolated execution and no replacement of Canvas or authoritative document
storage. Runtime behavior and verified capabilities remain documented in
[school.md](../school.md).
